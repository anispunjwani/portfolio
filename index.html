About
[PHOTO]

I work on problems that sit between systems, people, and decisions.

My work is grounded in a design-led way of thinking that has expanded well beyond design itself. Across product, UX, operations, consulting, and research, I help organizations navigate complexity, align around what matters, and make better decisions under real constraints.

How I think

I tend to think in systems before I think in outputs, paying attention to the underlying structure of a problem before jumping to solutions: how information moves, where incentives misalign, which assumptions are quietly shaping decisions, and what happens downstream when something scales.

Most organizations respond to uncertainty by adding more: more features, more process, more layers, more meetings. My instinct runs the other way. Reduce noise, find the governing logic, identify the constraint that matters most, and build only what meaningfully changes the system.

I'm especially attentive to the moments people avoid saying what they already know to be true: a team building something because of the momentum already behind it, a process that survives because nobody has stepped back far enough to question it. Recognizing those patterns early, and knowing when something is overdesigned versus when simplicity is the smarter move, is a big part of the work.

How I work

Moving between product, UX, operations, strategy, and research taught me that most problems are rarely isolated. A product issue is often a communication issue. A workflow issue is sometimes an incentive issue. A strategy problem can quietly become a systems problem.

Because of that, my role shifts with what the situation needs. Sometimes I'm leading research and shaping direction. Sometimes I'm redesigning an experience, facilitating alignment, or helping a team understand what should not be built in the first place.

Outside of work

I spend a lot of time thinking about cities, systems, culture, and how people make meaning together. I care about work that holds up in the real world, the kind that stays useful after the presentation ends.
